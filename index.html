Phil1009

Logic and Critical Thinking

An inquiry into the examination of arguments. This course introduces the fundamental concepts of logic and the methods of logical reasoning, building the skills to analyze, evaluate, and construct arguments.

Credit hours: 3 (ECTS)  ·  Level: Freshman, Semester I  ·  Academic year: 2025/2026, 1st Semester  ·  Instructor: Mohammed Z.  ·  Office: Block 80, Room 05

01 Course overview

Logic and Critical Thinking equips students with the essential skills to analyze, evaluate, and construct arguments. By engaging directly with the material, students cultivate intellectual curiosity and refine their ability to think critically, in both spoken and written expression.

02 Course learning outcomes

By the end of the course, students should be able to:

CLO 1

Define key philosophical and logical concepts, and differentiate between deductive and inductive reasoning.

CLO 2

Analyze arguments to identify logical fallacies and weaknesses in reasoning.

CLO 3

Construct sound, well-supported arguments and critique the arguments of others using logical principles.

CLO 4

Apply principles of critical thinking and linguistic precision to evaluate complex information and societal issues.
